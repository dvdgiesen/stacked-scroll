PagedScroll

A hardened page-turn scroll effect

Scroll down. Each section behaves like a page in a newspaper: read through the content while the header stays pinned, then the next page slides up to take its place.

This demo uses PagedScroll, a tiny GSAP ScrollTrigger wrapper extracted from a production site. It solves the two edge cases that break every naive implementation:

  • Short last panel — an invisible spacer guarantees the final section can scroll flush to the top, even when its content is shorter than the viewport.
  • Deep links — the robust scrollTo() calculation never walks the offsetParent chain, so pinned sections cannot corrupt the target position.
  • Constraint — two consecutive short panels are not supported in v1; this demo avoids them.

The page-turn mechanic

This section is taller than your viewport. Scroll through its body first. Only once you reach the bottom does the next section begin to slide over it.

That is the core difference between this effect and a simple sticky header. With sticky positioning alone, the header releases as soon as its container scrolls out. Here, the entire panel stays visually locked until you have consumed its content, producing a hard cut to the next section.

01 — Pin start logic

If a panel is shorter than the viewport, it pins immediately at start: "top top". If it is taller, it waits until its own bottom reaches the viewport bottom with start: "bottom bottom".

02 — pinSpacing: false

By disabling ScrollTrigger's default reserved scroll space, the next panel can slide up and cover the pinned one the moment the handoff begins.

03 — The offsetParent trap

When GSAP pins a panel, it sets position: fixed. That makes offsetTop and offsetParent unreliable for computing a target's document position — the classic deep-link bug. PagedScroll avoids them entirely.

04 — Natural height sum

offsetHeight is a panel's own natural size; fixed positioning changes its offset, never its size. Summing preceding panels gives the target panel's true document top.

05 — Same-tick rect diff

getBoundingClientRect() on the target and its panel share the same live viewport coordinate space. Their difference is the target's true offset within its panel.

06 — Header compensation

Each scrollTo() call subtracts the target section's own header height, so the pinned header never obscures the destination.

Visual rhythm

Mixing tall and short panels gives editorial rhythm without breaking the physics of the scroll. Tall panels are read; short panels are seen.

Because the spacer is sized generically against the last panel, reordering sections does not require code changes. The demo deliberately places a short panel last to prove the spacer fix.

Flush top

Because this last panel's content is shorter than the viewport, the browser cannot scroll it to the top on its own.
