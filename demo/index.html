StackedScroll

A hardened stacked-pages scroll effect

Scroll down. Each section behaves like a page in a stack of papers: read through the content while the header stays pinned, then the next page slides up to take its place.

This demo uses StackedScroll, a tiny GSAP ScrollTrigger wrapper extracted from rotyounges.com. It solves the edge cases that break every naive implementation:

  • Deep links — the robust scrollTo() calculation never walks the offsetParent chain, so pinned sections cannot corrupt the target position, whether you are linking to a section top or an element halfway down the page.
  • Short last panel — an invisible spacer guarantees the final section can scroll flush to the top, even when its content is shorter than the viewport.
  • Final transition — the previous panel's pin ends cleanly at the last transition, so headers never overlap.
  • Adjacent short panels — two short panels in a row now hand off cleanly thanks to a synthetic settle buffer, so you do not have to author around them.

The stacked-pages mechanic

This section is taller than your viewport. Scroll through its body first. Only once you reach the bottom does the next section begin to slide over it.

That is the core difference between this effect and a simple sticky header. With sticky positioning alone, the header releases as soon as its container scrolls out. Here, the entire panel stays visually locked until you have consumed its content, producing a hard cut to the next section.

How the deep-link fix works

01 — Pin start logic

If a panel is shorter than the viewport, it pins immediately at start: "top top". If it is taller, it waits until its own bottom reaches the viewport bottom with start: "bottom bottom".

02 — pinSpacing: false

By disabling ScrollTrigger's default reserved scroll space, the next panel can slide up and cover the pinned one the moment the handoff begins.

03 — The offsetParent trap

When GSAP pins a panel, it sets position: fixed. That makes offsetTop and offsetParent unreliable for computing a target's document position — the classic deep-link bug. StackedScroll avoids them entirely.

04 — Natural height sum

offsetHeight is a panel's own natural size; fixed positioning changes its offset, never its size. Summing preceding panels gives the target panel's true document top.

05 — Same-tick rect diff

getBoundingClientRect() on the target and its panel share the same live viewport coordinate space. Their difference is the target's true offset within its panel.

06 — Header compensation

Each scrollTo() call subtracts the target section's own header height, so the pinned header never obscures the destination.

Try it: open the ☰ menu and tap Gallery — that is a deep link to a section top.

Or jump straight to a mid-section element: 02 — pinSpacing: false, 03 — The offsetParent trap. The same `scrollTo()` calculation lands on the element, not just the section top, as long as there is enough content below it to scroll there.

Adjacent short section

This is the second short panel in a row. The previous one released before this one pinned, because an invisible settle buffer sits between them.

StackedScroll detects when two consecutive sections are both shorter than the viewport and injects a spacer with height viewportHeight − sectionHeight. That creates a full viewport of scroll distance for the handoff to complete.

Visual rhythm

Mixing tall and short panels gives editorial rhythm without breaking the physics of the scroll. Tall panels are read; short panels are seen.

The two short panels you scrolled through above are followed by this tall section. If they were placed back-to-back, the first pin would try to release at the same moment the second one engaged. StackedScroll inserted an invisible settle buffer between them so the handoff stays clean.

Flush top

Because this last panel's content is shorter than the viewport, the browser cannot scroll it to the top on its own.

StackedScroll adds an invisible spacer at the end of the document, sized to exactly the missing height. Try resizing your browser — the spacer recalculates on every resize.
